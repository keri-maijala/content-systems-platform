# Discovery Meeting Guide

This guide is for Keri's use during the initial client meeting. The goal of this meeting is to determine whether the Content Systems Platform is the right fit for the client — not to configure it. Configuration comes later.

The meeting should feel like a conversation, not an interview. Use these questions as a framework, not a script. Listen for signals that reveal expectations, organizational readiness, and content maturity.

At the end of the meeting, Keri makes one of three calls:
- **Good fit** — proceed to the client questionnaire
- **Yellow flag** — proceed with conditions or caveats; note what needs to be resolved
- **Not a fit** — decline clearly and, where possible, suggest what might serve them better

---

## Section 1: Understanding their content world

These questions establish what the client actually does with content — scope, volume, and complexity.

- What kinds of content does your organization produce? (Marketing, product UI, documentation, emails, social, other)
- Who creates content today — is there a dedicated content person or team, or is it distributed across functions?
- How does content get reviewed and approved before it goes out?
- What tools does your team use to create and manage content?

**Listen for:** Whether there is anyone who owns content, how distributed or centralized their process is, and whether they have any existing governance or review structure.

---

## Section 2: Understanding their pain

These questions surface why they're here and what they're hoping to solve.

- What's not working about how content gets created or managed right now?
- Where does content quality break down most often?
- Have you tried to put guidelines or standards in place before? What happened?
- What would "better" look like for your team?

**Listen for:** Whether the pain is real and specific (good fit signal) or vague and undefined (may not be ready). Whether they want order from chaos (good fit) or want to outsource thinking entirely (potential disqualifier).

---

## Section 3: Probing for disqualifiers

These questions surface expectations that may disqualify the client. Ask them naturally — not as a checklist.

**On compliance and legal:**
- Do you produce content that falls under regulatory or compliance requirements — healthcare, financial services, legal?
- If so, what does your current review process look like for that content?

*Disqualifier signal: Expects the platform to handle compliance without human review, or to absorb legal responsibility.*

**On human involvement:**
- How do you imagine your team interacting with this kind of tool day to day?
- Is there someone on your team who would own the guidelines and governance — or would you expect the tool to manage that autonomously?

*Disqualifier signal: Expects the platform to replace a content person entirely with no human in the loop.*

**On decision-making:**
- When there's a disagreement about brand or content direction, how does that get resolved today?
- Would you expect this tool to make those calls, or to inform them?

*Disqualifier signal: Expects the platform to make final brand or business decisions.*

**On governance appetite:**
- How does your team typically respond to content guidelines — are they embraced, ignored, or somewhere in between?
- Is leadership aligned on the value of content standards?

*Disqualifier signal: No appetite for any governance layer; leadership resistance to creating order.*

**On engagement model:**
- How are you thinking about the setup process — do you have someone internally who would configure and manage a tool like this, or are you looking for a more guided approach?

*Yellow flag signal: Expects self-service under the current engagement model. Note for future self-service tier.*

---

## Section 4: Organizational readiness

These questions assess whether the client's structure can support the platform.

- Who would be the primary point of contact for this engagement — someone who owns content or has authority over content decisions?
- Are there different teams or functions that produce content independently? How do they relate to each other?
- Who would need to be involved in setting this up — just you, or others?

**Listen for:** Whether there is a viable content owner/admin, whether domains are definable, and whether the right people are in the room (or can be).

---

## Section 5: Expectations and timeline

These questions surface practical fit.

- What's driving the timing on this — is there a specific event, launch, or pain point creating urgency?
- What does success look like six months after this is in place?
- Are there constraints we should know about — budget, timeline, team capacity for a setup engagement?

**Listen for:** Whether expectations are realistic, whether there is genuine commitment to the engagement process, and whether success is defined in terms the platform can actually deliver.

---

## Fit assessment

After the meeting, use this framework to make the call.

### Hard disqualifiers — decline if any of these are present
- Expects compliance or legal responsibility to transfer to the platform
- Expects regulated content to be published without human review
- Expects the platform to replace a content person with no human in the loop
- Expects the platform to make final brand or business decisions
- No one in the organization owns content — no viable content owner/admin
- No defined or definable content domains
- Leadership unwilling to commit to a discovery engagement
- No appetite for any governance layer whatsoever

### Yellow flags — proceed with conditions
- Very small team where one person wears all roles — confirm they understand the workload
- Heavily regulated industry — confirm explicit human review is built into their process
- Strong existing brand guidelines — note likely voice and tone customization needed
- Expects self-service — note for future tier; clarify current engagement model
- Leadership acknowledges content chaos but hasn't fully committed to addressing it — get explicit commitment before proceeding

### Good fit signals
- Clear content pain with specific examples
- Someone who owns content or can own it
- Definable content domains with identifiable owners
- Leadership aligned on the value of content standards
- Realistic expectations about what the platform does and doesn't do
- Willingness to invest in a setup engagement

---

## After the meeting

- **Good fit:** Send the client questionnaire. Schedule a follow-up to walk through it together.
- **Yellow flag:** Document the flags. Decide whether to proceed with conditions or decline. If proceeding, address the flags explicitly before sending the questionnaire.
- **Not a fit:** Decline clearly. Where possible, suggest what might serve them better — a different tool, a different engagement model, or a recommendation to build internal capacity first.
